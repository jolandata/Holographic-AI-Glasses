# Holographic AI Glasses
**Glass Optics & Holographic Projection — Engineering Feasibility Brief**

*Preliminary engineering collaboration | November 2026*

### 1. Objective

We are exploring the development of lightweight, transparent glasses capable of displaying computer-generated holographic 3D imagery within the user's natural field of view.

The objective is to investigate whether the glass lenses, optical components and projection mechanisms can be integrated into a practical, manufacturable glasses-like structure.

We are seeking an engineer with expertise in glass manufacturing, mechanical design and product feasibility to help establish what can be built using existing technologies and what requires new development.

### 2. Proposed optical system

The intended system would combine several functional elements:

1. **Transparent optical glass or substrate:** The see-through element through which the wearer views the environment. Investigate suitable glass materials, thicknesses, coatings, surface treatments and fabrication methods.
2. **Holographic image generation:** A miniature light source and spatial light modulator (SLM), or an alternative optical engine, to generate the light modulation needed for reconstructing 3D imagery.
3. **Optical coupling and projection:** A waveguide, holographic optical element (HOE), diffractive structure or optical combiner that directs the modulated light toward the eye while preserving transparency.
4. **Pupil alignment and image delivery:** Optical elements that establish a usable viewing region. Eye-tracking-assisted pupil adaptation is a potential approach to investigate.
5. **Mechanical integration:** Precision mounting and alignment of the glass, optical engine, coupling elements and supporting components within a lightweight frame.

The central technical challenge is to establish a transparent optical path that can deliver **genuine holographic wavefront reconstruction**, rather than merely displaying a conventional flat or stereoscopic image on transparent glass.

### 3. Engineering feasibility questions

- Can existing glass substrates, waveguides, HOEs or combiners support the proposed optical configuration?
- How would light enter, propagate through or interact with the glass, and reach the eye?
- Where must the light source, SLM, coupling optics and pupil-management elements be positioned?
- Which components can be purchased, adapted or assembled, and which require custom fabrication?
- What glass-production processes, coatings, bonding methods and precision tolerances would be required?
- Can the components fit within a realistic glasses frame while maintaining optical alignment, acceptable weight and thermal stability?
- Which optical configurations warrant specialist simulation or laboratory verification before manufacturing?

### 4. Requested engineering contribution

We would particularly value assistance with:

- **Feasibility drawings:** Scaled cross-sections, 3D component layouts, optical-element positioning, mounting arrangements and assembly concepts.
- **Component identification:** Commercially available optical modules, glass components, miniature projection systems and potential suppliers.
- **Glass manufacturing assessment:** Material selection, precision fabrication, coatings, bonding, tolerances, inspection and manufacturing limitations.
- **Technology-gap analysis:** Identification of components requiring custom development, specialist optical design or new fabrication methods.
- **Prototype planning:** A preliminary bill of materials, alternative configurations, indicative costs and a practical route toward a laboratory demonstrator.

### 5. Initial design objectives

The wider project currently considers an aspirational glasses-only mass below 50 g, a 40–50° field of view, a 6–8 mm eyebox and 35–60 pixels per degree. These are **research targets, not verified specifications**. The first feasibility study should establish realistic performance and manufacturing constraints rather than assume these targets are achievable.

### 6. Intended outcome

The immediate objective is **not to manufacture complete holographic glasses**.

It is to identify one or two physically credible optical configurations, produce initial feasibility drawings, distinguish commercially available from custom components, and determine the critical technical questions requiring optical/photonics specialists.

This engineering foundation would support a subsequent laboratory proof of principle and a credible proposal for research or development funding.

**Proposed collaboration:** Joint exploration of glass optics, physical integration and manufacturing feasibility, with specialist photonics and holographic-rendering expertise introduced where necessary.
